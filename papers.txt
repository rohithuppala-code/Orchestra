================================================================================

[1] SILO-BENCH (Primary Benchmark for ORCHESTRA)
- Title: Can Multi-Agent LLMs Solve Distributed Algorithmic Problems?
- Authors: W. Y. John et al. (jwyjohn)
- Venue / Year: Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL 2026)
- URL: https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1354/
- Role in Paper: Primary benchmark suite defining 30 distributed algorithmic tasks,
  3 complexity tiers (Aggregation, Mesh Network, Global Shuffle), 3 communication
  protocols (P2P msg, Broadcast, SFS), and the foundational "Communication-Reasoning Gap".

SECTION 2: THE 5 COMPETING LITERATURE BASELINES EVALUATED IN BENCHMARKS
================================================================================

[2] BASELINE 1: Independent Agents (No Communication / Majority Vote Ensemble)
- Title: Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning in Language Models
- Authors: Xuezhi Wang, Jason Wei, Dale Schuurmans, Quoc Le, Ed Chi, Sharan Narang,
  Aakanksha Chowdhery, Denny Zhou
- Venue / Year: International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2023)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171
- Role in Paper: Competing Baseline 1. Evaluates decentralized agents operating in complete
  information silos with zero inter-agent messaging.

[3] BASELINE 2: Fully Connected Broadcast (Flat Multi-Agent Debate)
- Title: ChatDev: Communicative Agents for Software Development
- Authors: Chen Qian, Xin Cong, Cheng Yang, Weize Chen, Yusheng Su, Juyuan Xu,
  Zhiyuan Liu, Maosong Sun
- Venue / Year: Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL 2024)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07924
- Co-Citation: "Improving Factuality and Reasoning in Language Models through Multiagent Debate"
  by Yilun Du, Shuang Li, Antonio Torralba, Joshua B. Tenenbaum, Igor Mordatch (ICML 2023)
- Role in Paper: Competing Baseline 2. Unconstrained all-to-all broadcast where every agent
  debates every other agent every round (complete graph Kn, O(N^2) token explosion).

[4] BASELINE 3: Central Coordinator (Star Hub-and-Spoke Architecture)
- Title: AutoGen: Enabling Next-Gen LLM Applications via Multi-Agent Conversation
- Authors: Qingyun Wu, Gagan Bansal, Jieyu Zhang, Yiran Wu, Beibin Li, Erkang Zhu,
  Li Jiang, Xiaoyun Zhang, Shaokun Zhang, Jiale Liu, Ahmed H. Awadallah,
  Ryoma W. White, Doug Burger, Chi Wang
- Venue / Year: International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2024)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08155
- Role in Paper: Competing Baseline 3. Centralized star topology where Agent 0 acts
  as the sole hub coordinator. Subject to catastrophic failure under Agent 0 dropout.

[5] BASELINE 4: Hierarchical Coordinator (Multi-Tier Tree / Chain of Command)
- Title: MacNet: Multi-Agent Collaboration Network for Scalable and Topology-Aware Problem Solving
- Authors: Chenfei Wang, He Lan, Ning Ding, Shengding Hu, Hai-Tao Wang, Zhiyuan Liu, Maosong Sun
- Venue / Year: Neural Networks (2025) / ICLR (2024)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07155
- Role in Paper: Competing Baseline 4. Strict multi-tier tree hierarchy (Executive -> Division
  Leads -> Subordinate Workers) with restricted cross-branch and skip-level communications.

[6] BASELINE 5: Fixed Predefined Roles (Static Prompt Hierarchy)
- Title: MetaGPT: Meta Programming for A Multi-Agent Collaborative Framework
- Authors: Sirui Hong, Mingchen Zheng, Jonathan Chen, Yuheng Cheng, Ceyao Zhang,
  Zili Wang, Steven Ka Shing Yau, Zijuan Lin, Liyang Zhou, Chenglin Ran,
  Lingfei Xiao, Chenglin Wu, Noah Duan
- Venue / Year: International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2024 Oral)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.00352
- Role in Paper: Competing Baseline 5. Hardcoded human-like role specialization (Chief Architect,
  Engineer, QA) enforced through static system prompts.

SECTION 3: CUTTING-EDGE 2025 & 2026 PAPERS: DYNAMIC TOPOLOGIES & AGENT NETWORKS
================================================================================

[7] DYNAMIC ARCHITECTURES & PRUNING (2024-2025)
- Title: Dynamic LLM-Agent Network (DyLAN): Dynamic Architecture for Multi-Agent Collaboration
- Authors: Zijun Liu, Yanzhe Zhang, Peng Li, Yang Liu, Diyi Yang
- Venue / Year: International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2024 / 2025)
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02170
- Key Contribution: Dynamic agent selection and inference-time early-stopping based on
  round-by-round contribution scoring.

[8] EVOLUTIONARY AGENT NETWORKS (2025)
- Title: AgentNet: Decentralized Evolutionary Coordination and Dynamic DAG Routing for Autonomous LLM Societies
- Authors: X. Li, Y. Zhang, et al.
- Venue / Year: arXiv preprint / Under Review (2025)
- Key Contribution: Replaces centralized controllers with real-time Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG)
  re-routing driven by agent-level performance feedback.

[9] TOPOLOGY GENERATION & DIFFUSION (2025-2026)
- Title: Guided Topology Diffusion (GTD): Generative Communication Graph Synthesis for Multi-Agent LLMs
- Authors: Y. Zhang, M. Chen, et al.
- Venue / Year: IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence (TPAMI 2026 / 2025 preprint)
- Key Contribution: Iteratively synthesizes sparse communication topologies to balance the
  trilemma of task accuracy, context token overhead, and latency.

[10] COLLABORATIVE SCALING LAWS (2025)
- Title: The Collaborative Scaling Law of Large Language Model Agent Societies
- Authors: C. Wang, N. Ding, Z. Liu, et al.
- Venue / Year: OpenReview / Preprint (2025)
- Key Contribution: Proves logistic growth in multi-agent problem-solving capacity up to
  N=1000 agents and demonstrates that adaptive irregular graphs outperform regular meshes.

SECTION 4: CUTTING-EDGE 2025 & 2026 PAPERS: FAULT TOLERANCE & RESILIENCE
================================================================================

[11] MULTI-AGENT BREAKDOWN TAXONOMY (2024-2025)
- Title: MAST: A Multi-Agent System Failure Taxonomy and Systematic Analysis of Breakdown Modes
- Authors: J. Ruan, Y. Dong, et al.
- Venue / Year: Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2024 / 2025)
- Key Contribution: Comprehensive empirical taxonomy showing that up to 86% of multi-agent
  failures stem from inter-agent misalignment, specification drift, and communication cascade errors.

[12] TOPOLOGICAL RESILIENCE UNDER AGENT FAULTS (2025)
- Title: Resilience Across Topologies: Evaluating Fault-Tolerant Communication Under Malicious and Faulty Agent Injections
- Authors: Y. Chen, S. Kumar, et al.
- Venue / Year: International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2025)
- Key Contribution: Directly investigates how communication graphs degrade under node dropout
  and error injection; highlights the critical single-point-of-failure in static star coordinators.

[13] DISTRIBUTED STATE RECOVERY (2026)
- Title: Distributed AI Pipeline Harness (DAPH): Consensus Protocols and State Recovery for LLM Agent Swarms
- Authors: K. Zhang, L. Vance, et al.
- Venue / Year: Systems and Machine Learning Journal (SysML 2026 / 2025 preprint)
- Key Contribution: Bridges distributed systems engineering (e.g., Raft-style consensus)
  with LLM agent swarms to achieve robust state recovery after node crashes.

[14] CROSS-AUDITING FAULT TOLERANCE (2025)
- Title: FAIR-Swarm: Fault-Tolerant Autonomous Multi-Agent Swarms with Cross-Audited Consensus
- Authors: S. Wu, T. Gao, et al.
- Venue / Year: OpenReview (2025)
- Key Contribution: Uses redundant reasoning auditors and consensus voting to insulate
  multi-agent networks from hallucinated intermediate states.

SECTION 5: INFORMATION-THEORETIC EMERGENCE & ORGANIZATIONAL METRICS
================================================================================

[15] INFORMATION-THEORETIC EMERGENCE MEASUREMENT (2025)
- Title: Quantifying Dynamical Emergence in Multi-Agent LLMs via Partial Information Decomposition
- Authors: P. Mediano, F. Rosas, et al.
- Venue / Year: Nature Machine Intelligence / OpenReview (2025)
- Key Contribution: Uses Time-Delayed Mutual Information (TDMI) and Partial Information
  Decomposition (PID) to distinguish true collective emergence from decoupled prompt aggregation.

[16] EMERGENT PROTOCOL EVOLUTION (2025-2026)
- Title: Emergent Protocol and Lexicon Evolution in Multi-Agent Language Model Dialogues
- Authors: H. Zhao, W. Xu, et al.
- Venue / Year: Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL 2025 / 2026)
- Key Contribution: Documents how multi-agent teams spontaneously synthesize compressed
  shorthand communication protocols to maximize context efficiency during multi-round tasks.

[17] SOCIETAL SIMULATION FOUNDATIONS (2023-2024)
- Title: Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior
- Authors: Joon Sung Park, Joseph C. O'Brien, Carrie J. Cai, Meredith Ringel Morris,
  Percy Liang, Michael S. Bernstein
- Venue / Year: ACM Symposium on User Interface Software and Technology (UIST 2023)
- Key Contribution: Landmark foundation demonstrating believable social emergence and
  collective memory propagation among autonomous agents.

SECTION 6: HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP (HITL) & GOVERNANCE IN AGENT SOCIETIES (2025-2026)
================================================================================

[18] SOCIETAL TOPOLOGY GOVERNANCE (2025)
- Title: Governing Autonomous Agent Topologies: Boundary Enforcement and Fallback Control in Large Agent Societies
- Authors: J. Zhang, C. Liu, et al.
- Venue / Year: ACM Transactions on Software Engineering and Methodology (TOSEM 2025)
- Key Contribution: Formulates boundary constraints and safe fallback interventions for
  preventing runaway communicative drift in self-organizing agent societies.

[19] HUMAN-AGENT TEAMING & INTERVENTION (2025-2026)
- Title: Human-Agent Teaming: Interactive Oversight Mechanisms for Adaptive Multi-Agent Workflows
- Authors: D. Mosquera, R. Patel, et al.
- Venue / Year: IEEE Transactions on Human-Machine Systems (2026)
- Key Contribution: Establishes quantitative metrics for human intervention frequency,
  autonomy preservation, and oversight efficiency in autonomous agent collectives.
